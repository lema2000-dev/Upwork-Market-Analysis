$5,000: Read handwritten lottery tickets & compute payout totals (OCR+logic)
Posted yesterday
Worldwide

Summary
We need a system that reads photographed (not scanned) handwritten betting tickets from a numbers-lottery business and outputs a fully computed, structured version of each ticket.

The tickets: handwritten 3- and 4-digit numbers on small paper slips, with checkboxes for which lottery/draw was selected (NY, NJ, Brooklyn, etc. — midday/night). Templates vary, and some batches show multiple tickets photographed together rather than one ticket per photo — see attached samples for both styles.

The task: extract every played number, double it into "B"/"S" entries, price it per that ticket's own rule, sort, and total. The computed total must match a total the agent already writes by hand on the original — that's your built-in correctness check. Pricing rules are not fixed across tickets; you'll need to detect the rule (or the written anchor) per ticket, not assume one global formula.

Target: 100% exact match ideal, ≥90% field-level accuracy acceptable, across the ticket set.

Baseline: none working yet. Free OCR (Tesseract) fails outright on most sample tickets even after basic preprocessing.

Hard constraints (non-negotiable): if your solution calls paid APIs, total cost must stay under $40/day at our real volume (~3,000-4,000 tickets/day, rising). If it runs locally, it must fit on 5x NVIDIA DGX Spark units (128GB unified memory each). An accurate solution that breaks either limit is not usable.

Deliverable: working pipeline, documentation of the pricing rule(s) you found, and cost/throughput numbers proving it fits the constraints above.

Sample data: 2 example finished/result tickets attached here (shows the target output format). The full 200-ticket set (raw photos + originals, both styles) is shared privately once we start working together.

Budget: $5,000, paid on verified delivery. Timeline: ASAP.
